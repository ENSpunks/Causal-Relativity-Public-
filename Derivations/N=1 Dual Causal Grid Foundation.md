# N=1 Dual Causal Grid Foundation

## Status

Foundational formulation / research hypothesis.

This document formalizes the N=1 starting point of Causal Relativity as a **dual causal-grid system**. It is intentionally separated from established GR terminology where the correspondence has not yet been mathematically demonstrated.

The objective is to provide a precise starting model from which the existing derivations in this repository can be re-derived or falsified.

---

## 1. N=1 universe

The N=1 universe contains one physical mass-energy system, denoted by \(M\), with no external physical reference object.

The intrinsic frame of \(M\) is defined by the condition

\[
\mathbf{x}_M = 0.
\]

Therefore the mass is always at rest in its own relative frame:

\[
v_{M/M}=0.
\]

This is not a claim that relative velocity is unphysical. It is a statement about where relative velocity is represented.

In Causal Relativity, relative velocity is represented by the transformation/distortion of the causal structures associated with the measured system rather than by motion of the system through its own intrinsic frame.

---

## 2. The causal sphere

The mass generates a causal sphere containing two independent causal grids:

\[
M \longrightarrow \{G,L\}
\]

where

- \(G\) is the **gravity causal grid**;
- \(L\) is the **light causal grid**.

The grids share an invariant propagation speed \(c\), but they are not assumed to be the same field, to contain the same information, or to obey the same interaction equations.

The common propagation speed is therefore a postulate to be explained by the underlying causal geometry, not evidence that the two grids are identical.

---

## 3. Intrinsic radial propagation

Let \(\sigma\) denote an intrinsic causal-order parameter. It is introduced as an ordering parameter and is not initially identified with physical clock time.

For an undistorted N=1 system, the causal radius of either grid satisfies

\[
\frac{dr_G}{d\sigma}=c,
\qquad
\frac{dr_L}{d\sigma}=c.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
r_G=c\sigma,
\qquad
r_L=c\sigma,
\]

up to the choice of origin.

The source remains at the center while the causal structures propagate outward. Thus propagation of the grids does not require translation of the source mass through its own intrinsic spatial frame.

---

## 4. Independent information carried by the grids

The two grids carry different causal information.

### 4.1 Gravity grid

A gravity shell at causal radius \(r\) represents the gravitational state causally established by the source state from which that shell propagated.

Write this schematically as

\[
G(r)=\mathcal{G}[M(\sigma_G(r))].
\]

The notation emphasizes that the gravitational state at a location is not necessarily the instantaneous state of the source. It is information that has propagated causally from an earlier source state in the intrinsic ordering.

The exact relationship between \(G\), gravitational acceleration, metric geometry, and spacetime curvature remains to be derived.

In particular,

\[
\frac{GM}{r^2}
\]

should initially be treated as gravitational field strength/acceleration rather than being identified directly with a geometric curvature scalar or tensor.

### 4.2 Light grid

A light shell is associated with an emission event/state of the source. If a light shell is emitted when the source is in causal state \(M(\sigma_e)\), its information can be represented schematically as

\[
L(r;\sigma_e)=\mathcal{L}[M(\sigma_e),E_e],
\]

where \(E_e\) denotes additional emission information such as frequency, direction, polarization, phase, or other relevant state variables.

Consequently, a light shell and a gravity shell occupying the same causal radius need not contain equivalent information about the source.

---

## 5. Relative frames

Consider another frame \(F'\) with relative velocity \(v\) with respect to the intrinsic frame of \(M\).

The fundamental N=1 statement remains

\[
\mathbf{x}_M=0
\]

in the intrinsic frame of the measured mass.

Instead of representing the relative motion by translating \(M\) through its own frame, Causal Relativity represents the frame relationship through transformed causal grids:

\[
G \rightarrow G_v,
\qquad
L \rightarrow L_v.
\]

Thus the conceptual correspondence is

\[
\boxed{\text{relative velocity}\rightarrow\text{causal-grid distortion}}.
\]

The object being measured remains at rest in its own relative frame while the causal structures used to describe and measure it are distorted relative to another frame.

---

## 6. The grids need not transform identically

Because gravity and light are independent causal grids, their velocity-dependent transformations should initially be represented separately:

\[
G_v=T_G(v)G,
\]

\[
L_v=T_L(v)L.
\]

There is no foundational assumption that

\[
T_G(v)=T_L(v).
\]

The theory must determine whether a common underlying causal transformation produces the observed relativistic behavior while the two grids retain different physical interaction rules.

This distinction is essential. Causal Relativity is not proposing that gravity and light are simply two names for one field.

---

## 7. Geometry generated by the gravity grid

A central hypothesis is that the gravity causal grid participates in defining the spatial geometry through which other causal phenomena propagate.

Schematically,

\[
M \rightarrow G \rightarrow g_{ij} \rightarrow L.
\]

Here \(g_{ij}\) represents a spatial metric or effective spatial geometry derived from the gravitational causal state.

The light grid remains an independent causal structure, but its trajectories may depend on the geometry established by the gravity grid.

This provides a natural place to investigate gravitational lensing without identifying the light grid itself with gravity.

A successful derivation must recover the appropriate weak-field and relativistic limits rather than simply assume the standard GR geodesic equation.

---

## 8. Velocity as observable grid distortion

An observer does not directly observe an abstract velocity. The observer measures physical relationships: positions, intervals, frequencies, trajectories, clock rates, or other observables.

Under the N=1 interpretation, these observations can be represented as measurements of the transformed causal structures.

The research program is therefore to derive an observable transformation of the form

\[
\mathcal{O}'=\mathcal{T}_v(\mathcal{O};G,L),
\]

where \(\mathcal{O}\) denotes an observable constructed from interactions with the causal grids.

The key test is whether the resulting transformation necessarily contains the Lorentz factor

\[
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}
\]

without inserting \(\gamma\) as an independent postulate.

If the Lorentz factor can be derived from the causal-grid geometry, then length contraction, relativistic frequency shifts, and time-dilation measurements can be investigated as consequences rather than foundations.

---

## 9. Causal shells as distributed source history

The causal sphere provides a spatially distributed record of source states.

A sequence of gravity shells can be represented as

\[
G_1,G_2,G_3,\ldots
\]

and a sequence of light shells as

\[
L_1,L_2,L_3,\ldots.
\]

The N=1 causal state can therefore be represented schematically as

\[
U_1=\{M,G_1,G_2,\ldots,L_1,L_2,\ldots\}.
\]

This suggests that what is conventionally described as a history evolving through time may instead be represented as information distributed across causal shells.

This does **not** yet prove that physical time is nonexistent. The stronger and more useful research question is whether conventional time can be derived as an effective parameter describing ordered causal relationships between states and observations.

---

## 10. Minimum N=1 model

The minimum mathematical model to be developed is therefore

\[
\boxed{
U_1=(M,G,L,g_{ij},\mathcal{I})
}
\]

where

- \(M\) = central mass-energy state;
- \(G\) = gravity causal grid;
- \(L\) = light causal grid;
- \(g_{ij}\) = geometry induced or constrained by the causal state of \(G\);
- \(\mathcal{I}\) = interaction/measurement rules.

The model must satisfy at minimum:

1. \(M\) is at rest in its intrinsic frame.
2. Both causal grids propagate at invariant speed \(c\).
3. Gravity and light grids carry distinguishable information.
4. Gravity and light grids obey distinguishable interaction rules.
5. Relative velocity is represented by a transformation/distortion of the causal grids.
6. Observable predictions are independent of arbitrary coordinate labeling.
7. The appropriate Newtonian, SR, and GR limits are recovered where applicable.

---

## 11. Research sequence

The derivation program should proceed in the following order.

### Step 1 — N=1 geometry

Define the intrinsic frame and the mathematical meaning of the outward/right-angle causal propagation.

### Step 2 — Dual-grid state equations

Derive separate propagation/state equations for \(G\) and \(L\).

### Step 3 — Gravity geometry

Determine whether the inverse-square gravitational law and an effective metric follow from the geometry of \(G\), rather than being independently assumed.

### Step 4 — Light propagation

Derive the propagation of \(L\) through the geometry associated with \(G\), including the conditions under which trajectories bend around gravitating systems.

### Step 5 — Relative-frame transformation

Derive \(T_G(v)\) and \(T_L(v)\) from the intrinsic causal geometry.

### Step 6 — Recover relativity

Determine whether the observed Lorentz transformation and relativistic observables emerge from the grid transformations.

### Step 7 — Novel predictions

Identify predictions that differ from standard GR/SR and can be experimentally or observationally tested.

## 12. Falsifiability requirements

The dual-grid interpretation should not be treated as established physics merely because it reproduces known equations.

A successful theory must ultimately provide:

- mathematically well-defined state variables;
- dimensional consistency;
- conservation laws;
- a clear relationship to established gravitational geometry;
- recovery of established experimental limits;
- at least one nontrivial prediction that can distinguish Causal Relativity from existing theories.

The most important near-term objective is therefore a derivation of the relative-frame transformation from the N=1 dual-grid geometry.

---

## 13. Foundational statement

The central N=1 hypothesis can be summarized as:

> **An object is always at rest in its own intrinsic relative frame. Relative velocity is expressed not as motion of the object through itself, but as a distortion of the causal grids that relate that object to other frames.**

The causal sphere contains two independent grids—gravity and light—which propagate from the source at the invariant causal speed \(c\), while carrying different information and obeying different physical interaction rules.

The purpose of the N=1 program is to determine whether the known structure of relativity and gravity can be derived from this dual causal geometry rather than assumed at the outset.
